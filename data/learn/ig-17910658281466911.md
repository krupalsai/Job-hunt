# Learn for: Wipro — Graduate Engineer Trainee

## What this job actually is

A Graduate Engineer Trainee is Wipro's fresher intake. Read the word
"trainee" literally: they hire you for potential and train you on their
projects afterwards. The interview checks three things — can you do basic
aptitude, can you write basic code, and can you learn. They expect you to
arrive trainable, not trained.

## The honest starting point

You run a live platform with real users — that already puts you ahead of most
applicants. You integrated payments, databases and bots into a working system
and you debug it live. This role is for people who can learn, and you've
proven you can. Now you're filling in the programming fundamentals underneath
it all: writing the code yourself, not just wiring it together. Give yourself
8–12 weeks of daily practice.

## Learn in this order (simplest first)

### 1. What a program even is (week 1)

- You operate programs every day — now you're learning to write them. A
  program is a list of instructions the computer follows in order, like a
  recipe.
- Nothing to install: open **programiz.com/python** on your phone browser —
  free, runs instantly.
- First win: print your name on screen. That's a real program.

### 2. Python basics (weeks 2–4)

- **print**, **variables** (a named box holding a value), **input** (ask the
  user to type), **if / else** (decisions), **loops** (repeating).
- Golden rule: type every example yourself, never copy-paste. Then change it
  until it breaks. Fixing the break is the lesson.

### 3. Small problems (weeks 4–6)

- Even or odd. Biggest of three numbers. Star patterns. Reverse a word.
- Wipro's coding round is full of exactly these. Solve 3–5 a day.

### 4. Aptitude (30 minutes daily, alongside everything)

- Percentages, ratios, time & work, number series, basic logical reasoning.
- Always with a timer. Speed is scored, not just correctness.

### 5. Databases — SQL basics (weeks 6–8)

- A database is where apps remember things — like your student records.
- Learn **SELECT**, **WHERE**, **ORDER BY**, and **JOIN** (combining two
  tables). Free practice: **sqlbolt.com**.

### 6. Speak like a trainee (week 8+)

- Learn to explain, in your own words: what an API is, what a database is,
  what "testing" means. One paragraph each, from memory.
- Your 60-second project story: what your student platform does, what you
  wired together and operate yourself, one live issue you debugged. Rehearse
  on camera three times.
- Trainee interviews select for attitude as much as knowledge. Sounding
  honest and eager beats sounding rehearsed.

## How you'll know you're ready

- Timed aptitude: 70%+ correct, consistently.
- Easy coding problems solved without peeking at answers.
- You can explain "what is an API" in plain words to a friend.

## What NOT to worry about yet

Frameworks, cloud certificates, hard coding problems. Wipro trains you on
their stack AFTER hiring. The interview is fundamentals plus attitude.
